# The person and the moment

Ines Duarte is fictional, and so is Ledgerly, her small invoicing app. She is a solo developer in
Lisbon. Hermes runs on a mini PC under her desk and talks to her on Telegram. Her job "Nightly
release" fires at 02:00: update dependencies, run the tests, bump the patch version, publish, deploy.

At 02:14 on a Tuesday the tests pass and the next step is `npm publish`.

Without Abeyance, the step runs while she sleeps, or, if a step is one Hermes's detector flags, the
default cron setting blocks it with nobody to ask. With Abeyance the run stops before `npm publish`,
keeps steps 1 to 3, and one card reaches her phone. At 07:40 she replies `resume K7Q2`. Abeyance checks
the repo is the one she was shown. Hermes asks her to confirm `npm publish`. She taps once.

She is the only person who can say yes to publishing under her name. Abeyance makes that yes count for
that step, in that repo state, once.

No market-size figure is given and no incident is claimed as Hermes's. Interview: none received yet.
